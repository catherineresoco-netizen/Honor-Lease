VETERAN HOUSING RESOURCE GUIDE

Find support for the housing situation you're facing.

The VA-funded Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF) program supports eligible low-income veteran households experiencing homelessness or at imminent risk. Services can include housing search, case management, prevention assistance, and temporary financial help such as rent or deposits, subject to local eligibility and funds. These are referrals, not guaranteed grants.

Understanding financial assistance

SSVF is not an automatically awarded cash grant. Participating nonprofit providers determine eligibility and assistance case by case. Some organizations provide housing navigation or shelter rather than direct deposit funding.

Honor Flex's installment options and nonprofit support are different: one changes payment timing with a landlord's consent; the other may provide outside support to qualifying veterans.

Privacy: This demo does not request or store military records, income details, or application information.

Honor Flex · Business model

Affordability for renters.
Infrastructure for platforms.

Honor Flex is a proposed B2B API service. Platforms license the technology, landlords control deposit and concession policies, and renters see transparent offers in their existing search experience.

BUSINESS STRUCTUREB2B SaaS

For-profit software infrastructure rather than a rental marketplace or lender.

REVENUE SOURCEPartner contracts

Negotiated recurring licensing, optional usage and integration/service fees.

RENTER PLATFORM FEE$0

Honor Flex does not bill renters for viewing policies in this concept.

How value moves through the ecosystem

Rental software partnersLicense the API and integrate policy and disclosure components into existing platforms.
→
Participating landlordsChoose full deposit, upfront deposit, repayment plan and rent credit; retain collection responsibility.
→
RentersCompare move-in cash requirements and see future obligations before applying.
Revenue flows from software partners to Honor Lease through negotiated commercial agreements—not from security deposits or lender interest. Landlords fund any advertised rent concessions.

Why a platform might license

  • Access configurable deposit installments and rent-special disclosures without building every policy-management interface in-house.
  • Display consistent move-in-cost information across properties and channels.
  • Enable participating landlords to manage offers in an established workflow.
  • Evaluate the feature in a controlled pilot before commercial rollout.
These are proposed value drivers; cost savings, integration ease and partner demand have not yet been validated.

What makes us distinct?

Obligo and other existing providers already support deposit installments or alternatives. Honor Flex proposes a narrower, vendor-neutral policy and disclosure layer with **no financing or repayment guarantee**.

  • Upside: less capital exposure and transparent landlord-controlled terms.
  • Trade-off: landlords remain at risk for uncollected installments.
  • Challenge: software platforms may build similar features internally.

Partners & resources

  • Rental marketplaces and property-management technology providers
  • Participating property managers and landlords
  • Housing-law advisors, API engineers, hosting and secure data storage
  • Technical support, integration documentation and privacy safeguards

Operating model

  • Partner contract defines scope, support, integrations and negotiated fees.
  • Honor Lease maintains versioned deposit and concession policy APIs.
  • Platform displays costs, disclosures and installment timelines.
  • Landlords handle rent collection, deposit collection, and tenant decisions.

Path to a credible pilot

01 · Prototype

Interactive simulated marketplace + configurable deposit policies.

02 · Validate

Interview landlords, software buyers and renters; test pain points.

03 · Pilot

Obtain partner authorization, legal review and limited deployment.

04 · Expand

Measure adoption, cash deferred, integration effort and contract economics.
